## Confirmed Business Positioning

- Strategic Operations Solutions is positioned as an operational excellence and cost optimization partner.
- The business has two broad sides:
  - Operational excellence/process engineering: fee-for-service consulting.
  - Spend optimization/cost savings: performance-based work with no upfront cost.
- The core promise for spend optimization is not "we guarantee savings." The safer promise is access to specialist review, benchmarking, and third-party validation of spend opportunities.
- Outreach should emphasize helping companies run better and spend less, especially by finding working capital already inside the business before taking on debt or new funding.

## Target Client Rules

- Primary buyer/sponsor: CFO.
- Acceptable alternatives: business owner, CEO in smaller companies, controller only when there is no CFO.
- Middle-market companies are the main target.
- Revenue sweet spot: roughly $15 million to $750 million in gross revenue.
- Strong default target: $20 million or more in gross revenue.
- $15 million is the general floor, but spend profile matters more than revenue alone.
- Restaurant groups can be viable below the normal revenue floor if food spend is large enough.
- A rough employee-count proxy for private-company sizing is 50-80+ employees when revenue is unavailable, but revenue/spend data is preferred.
- The company should have meaningful indirect spend.
- Multiple locations are a positive signal because duplication and waste are more likely.
- United States prospects are acceptable, with continental United States preferred.
- Private and public companies are both acceptable.
- Municipalities are possible but lower priority because red tape can slow or block implementation.

## Prospect Sources and Research Workflow

- Current strongest prospect source: LinkedIn, especially posts and announcements that surface companies or business events.
- ZoomInfo is a second major source, especially weekly intent lists and company-level enrichment.
- Referrals and relationship introductions are high-value sources.
- Other useful sources include CBIA, Manufacturer CT newsletters, industry newsletters, podcasts, company news, and networking.
- LinkedIn Sales Navigator has saved searches by industry category, but its value versus standard LinkedIn and ZoomInfo should be reviewed.
- ZoomInfo intent terms include cost optimization, cost reduction, and expense reduction.
- HubSpot is the current contact-list system.
- Athena is the ERA Group database and is used to tag accounts so others do not pursue them, but some data may be sparse or dated.
- Research usually starts with a company, then the CFO/owner/contact.
- Research sequence:
  - Identify the company through LinkedIn, ZoomInfo, referral, news, or industry source.
  - Check LinkedIn for the CFO or owner.
  - Check for mutual connections and pursue warm introductions first.
  - Use ZoomInfo for revenue/company enrichment.
  - Check the company website to validate leadership/contact data because ZoomInfo can be stale.
  - Review company news, hiring, acquisitions, expansions, tariffs, industry pressure, and other topical hooks.
  - Use topical research to craft a relevant opening message and include a useful asset such as a newsletter or white paper when appropriate.

## Outreach Messaging Rules

- Do not promise savings.
- Do not say a specific company is overspending unless validated by actual spend data.
- Avoid specific savings numbers in cold outreach.
- Acceptable broad phrasing: history of finding double-digit savings, where appropriate.
- Emphasize third-party validation, access to subject-matter experts, and visibility into whether spend is optimized.
- Emphasize that the complimentary assessment is no cost and no obligation.
- Emphasize that the assessment is directional until real data is reviewed.
- Emphasize that the customer stays in control over which opportunities are pursued.
- Emphasize no upfront cost for the spend optimization side.
- Emphasize that fees are based on realized/measured savings after implementation, not merely projected savings.
- Do not position the assessment as a manufactured project. If there is no opportunity, the conversation ends.

## Spend Assessment Process

- The first step is usually a conversation and demonstration of the spend view/cost assessment platform.
- The prospect provides accounts payable spend data.
- Data request: roughly 12 months of recent historical AP spend.
- Salaries/headcount should be removed.
- Helpful fields include vendor name, cost, notes/description, company category, timing/date, and location.
- Prospect uploads the data into a secure portal.
- The team benchmarks spend against experience and historical project data.
- Assessment output categorizes spend into:
  - Actionable categories: high confidence that expertise can reduce costs.
  - Discoverable categories: possible opportunity, but more information needed.
  - Low-value categories: not enough opportunity to justify effort.
  - Static/non-addressable categories: taxes, tariffs, regulated/non-negotiable spend.
- Assessment turnaround after data upload: roughly two to three weeks.
- If the customer chooses to pursue an opportunity, that moves into project phase and engagement agreement.
